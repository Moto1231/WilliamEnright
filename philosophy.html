The Enright Core Assumptions

The foundational beliefs that guide every recommendation, every decision, and every solution

→

Assumption One

Business First

Technology decisions should begin with learning the business — its objectives, challenges, processes, and opportunities

Before discussing software, AI, ERP, or technology, first understand what makes the business successful

There is one question that matters:

Is it good for the business?

← →

Assumption Two

Business Outcomes Matter

Technology is valuable when it creates measurable business improvement

  • Reducing costs
  • Reducing risk
  • Improving productivity
  • Increasing quality and throughput
  • Creating revenue opportunities

The measure is the outcome

The measure is not the technology itself

← →

Assumption Three

Understand Before Recommend

Every business is unique because every business has different goals, challenges, processes, customers, and competitive advantages

The right technology decision cannot begin with:

What software should we implement?

Understanding must come before a recommendation

← →

Assumption Four

Competitive Advantages Win

Every successful business has something that makes it different

  • A process
  • A capability
  • A relationship
  • A way of serving customers
  • A manufacturing advantage

Technology decisions should protect and strengthen those advantages

← →

Assumption Five

Outcome Over Opinion

Opinions are everywhere. Trends come and go. Technology changes constantly

Technology decisions should not be driven by the loudest voice, the newest idea, or what everyone else is doing

What outcome are we trying to achieve?

The right technology decision is the one that produces the right business outcome

← →

Assumption Six

Take Responsibility

Recommendations are easy. Ownership is harder

A technology decision does not end when the plan is delivered, the software is selected, or the project goes live

I owned the consequences
